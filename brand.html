A club starts
with a hello.

A first brand and experience concept for Rachel Feldberg. Built around one belief: movement can give people a reason to meet. Thoughtful hosting helps them actually do it.

01 / The position

Make the first hello easier.

KIN brings people together through movement and wellness, but the ultimate goal is human connection. The workout, Pilates class, run, hike, coffee or social event is the vehicle for people to meet, connect and build relationships.

The person we’re here for

Anyone who wants more people in their life. They might be new to Ottawa, or simply ready for a wider circle. They like the idea of going, but feel unsure about arriving alone.

The promise we can keep

You will be welcomed and given an easy way to meet someone. We cannot promise a best friend. We can promise that making the first introduction won’t be left entirely to you.

Mission: Make it easier for people to build connections through movement.

KIN = your people.

Traditionally, kin means family, the people you are closely connected to.

  • KIN = family. Your people, your community, your sense of belonging.
  • KIN = kinesthetic. Movement, the body, energy, and physical connection.
  • KIN = connection. Meeting new people, building relationships, and shared experiences.

Brand line: Come find your KIN.

02 / The difference has to be delivered

Hosting is the product.

“Pilates and community” is already offered locally. uoPILATES, for example, explicitly combines Pilates with friendship and creative events. KIN’s opportunity is a consistent, carefully hosted arrival and a reason to return. This is a positioning hypothesis to test, not an “Ottawa’s only” claim. Source: uOttawa

What should stay constant

  • 16–24 guests, with capacity tested against Rachel’s ability to welcome everyone.
  • A named host, a real introduction, and a simple paired welcome.
  • A well-taught class that works without partner exercises.
  • Unhurried social time, guided in small groups.
  • A recognisable format that guests can describe to a friend.

What makes this hard to sustain

  • Pairing alone is easy to copy. Reliable hosting and repeat attendance must become the advantage.
  • An awkward match can limit the visit. Move pairs into groups of four after class.
  • Regulars can create a closed circle. Keep welcoming first-timers into the mix.
  • Rachel cannot greet late arrivals while teaching. Trial a welcome helper before increasing capacity.
  • Connection needs time. Don’t let a packed class schedule squeeze out the social part.

03 / The first offer

Pilates & good company.

One repeatable starting format. The times below are proposed for a pilot, not confirmed event details.

MomentThe experience
15 minutes
Arrive		Rachel welcomes each guest and introduces pairs. Offer one light prompt: “What’s something you’re looking forward to this week?” No personal disclosures required.
45 minutes
Move		A Rachel-led mat Pilates class. Pairs can place mats beside each other. No contact, partner choreography, or forced participation.
30 minutes
Stay		Join two pairs into a group of four. Give guests a place to sit and time to talk. Refreshments are optional, subject to venue and budget.
Before leaving
Continue		Let guests know when they can meet again, once the next event is confirmed. Contact exchanges stay voluntary. Offer a feedback invitation.

The reason to return

Start with a regular time and recognisable format. Returning guests meet a mix of familiar and new people. Later, repeat the format in different settings without losing the welcome.

The expansion filter

A new wellness activity earns a place only if Rachel can deliver it well and it makes conversation easier. Prove this first gathering before adding retreats, memberships, or a full menu of events.

Still open: date, venue, budget, ticket price, class level, equipment, access needs, and any refreshments. Price from real costs and Rachel’s paid time at 16 guests, not a sellout of 24.

04 / Visual identity

Warm invitation.
Clear, confident design.

The identity starts with the palette: a fresh lime, a soft lilac and a calm stone grey, grounded by very dark ink, paper and plum. Simple layouts and a single type family keep KIN easy to follow. The proposed lowercase wordmark and paired-circle motif bring the idea of familiarity into the system.

How the colors work

Lime opens the door: hero moments, highlights and headlines on dark grounds. Lilac marks events. Stone and paper give longer sections room. Ink makes the words direct. Plum adds depth for calmer, closing moments. This is a creative rationale, not a claim that colors cause a particular emotion.

Keep it readable

Use ink text on lime, lilac, stone or paper. Use lime or paper on ink and plum. Never set paper or white text on lime or lilac. Color should never be the only way to communicate a choice or state.

Wordmark: lowercase kin in heavy DM Sans, with a small CLUB descriptor. It is a type-based concept, not a custom-drawn logo. Two overlapping circles act as a small supporting motif for an introduction.

05 / Typography & photography

One voice. Room to read.

06 / The words

Write like a good host.

Warm, direct, and specific. Explain what happens instead of asking visitors to believe in an abstract community. Never label the visitor lonely, promise friendship, or imply she needs to become someone else to belong.

07 / Earn a second visit

Measure the connection.

Being a must-go event is earned through the experience and who returns. It cannot be designed into a logo or created by making every gathering a different novelty.

  • After the first pilot: ask whether guests felt welcomed and met someone they would like to see again.
  • At the next event: track how many first-event guests return. Record who heard about it from a past guest.
  • At every event: check whether newcomers received an introduction, and whether guests chose to stay after class.
  • Before adding dates: confirm demand, positive event margins including Rachel’s time, and reliable hosting.

Keep a simple arrival and hosting checklist as Rachel learns what works. That lets another host deliver the same care later. Grow the KIN guest list with consent, so demand belongs to the club as well as the founder.

08 / Research & open decisions

A focused starting point.

Research completed on 7 October 2026. This is a small landscape check, not proof that the market is saturated.

  • Habit Training FAQ: explicit class structure; use plain labels for KIN. Accordion answers were not verifiable in the research fetch.
  • uoPILATES: a student-focused Pilates community with themed formats and friendship as part of the offer.
  • Ottawa Social Girl Club: women’s social events are an alternative way to meet people.
  • Women of Colour Remake Wellness: wellness events and gatherings for Black, Indigenous and women of colour.
  • Sisters Fitness Club: women’s movement and community through a free weekly run club.
  • The BIOM: broader social wellness and movement in Ottawa.

Name to check: Kin Club of Orleans already operates locally. This may affect search clarity. “KIN Club” remains the working name; no name, domain, handle, or trademark clearance has been completed.

Next step for Rachel: review the words and visual direction, then cost one pilot gathering. Keep the first offer focused while we test whether guests feel welcomed and want to return.
